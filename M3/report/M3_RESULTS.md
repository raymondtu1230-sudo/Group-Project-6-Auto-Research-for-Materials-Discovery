## Why (interpretation, edit by hand)

Runs: 72 of 72 completed on the first attempt (21 Oct, one Windows PC, 3 runs at a time with 5 CPU threads each, same code revision `81b0e36` plus `M3/install.py --install` for every arm). No run failed, resumed from a checkpoint or shared a checkpoint folder. The re-run `m1_baseline` matches M1's recorded 19 Oct values exactly in all 18 pairs (mSUN and AUDC), so the reference is reproducible and turning on M4 memory does not change it.

**Overall.** `m3_full` finds 1.5 more stable-unique-novel structures per run than the M1 reference (6.50 vs 5.00), better in 9 of 18 pairs and worse in 6. The spread of the paired difference (±5.55) is larger than the mean, so this is not a reliable average gain. The effect depends strongly on the system:

| System | `m1_baseline` | `m3_full` | `m3_planning_off` | `m3_self_correction_off` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Au-K-Tb | 0.33 | 10.00 | 3.33 | 10.00 |
| Co-Dy-W | 1.67 | 5.67 | 1.67 | 5.67 |
| Co-Mg-Na | 1.33 | 4.33 | 3.67 | 1.00 |
| Ga-Pt-Tm | 7.33 | 6.00 | 7.33 | 6.33 |
| Hf-Ni-Zr | 11.67 | 6.00 | 9.33 | 5.67 |
| Mg-Sn-Sr | 7.67 | 7.00 | 5.33 | 6.33 |

(mean mSUN@20 over seeds 17/29/43)

M3 helps most on the three systems where the reference finds almost nothing (Au-K-Tb, Co-Dy-W, Co-Mg-Na: 1.1 → 6.7 on average) and loses on the three where it already does well (Ga-Pt-Tm, Hf-Ni-Zr, Mg-Sn-Sr: 8.9 → 6.3). M3 raises the floor and lowers the ceiling; its run-to-run spread is smaller (±2.60 vs ±4.41).

**Planning (+1.39, better in 12 of 18).** The planner's review log shows why the effect splits by system. It scores sub-goals from observed stable results and then commits to binary edges: in every `m3_full` run, 3–5 of the 5 four-query batches go to binary edges (Au-Tb/Au-K in Au-K-Tb, Ni-Zr in Hf-Ni-Zr), and the ternary interior gets at most 2 batches (none in 6 of 18 runs). When the stable phases sit on that edge, this concentrates the budget where discoveries are (Au-K-Tb: 10.0 vs 3.3 without planning). When the ternary interior is rich, as in Hf-Ni-Zr, the narrowing costs discoveries that the reference's full-system sampling finds (6.0 vs 11.7). The planner exploits too early; a minimum share of queries for the ternary interior, or a larger exploration bonus, is the obvious next step. It was not tuned here, because tuning on dev results would bias the comparison.

**Self-correction (+0.67, better in 7, same in 10, worse in 1).** The progress-signal fix worked. Before it, self-correction never fired with planning on and the two arms were identical in all 18 pairs. Now `m3_full` re-plans 16 times, all for no progress (no new stable phase in 6 queries); the repetition trigger never fires with the planner on, because the planner does not repeat formulas. In the 10 pairs with no difference, 7 runs never stalled long enough to trigger it, 2 re-planned too late to matter (queries 16–19 in Co-Dy-W seeds 29 and 43), and once (Hf-Ni-Zr seed 43) the re-plan found nothing new either. The clearest case is Co-Mg-Na: re-plans at queries 6 and 13 move the search off unproductive compositions (for seed 17, `Na10 Mg9 Co1` → `Na7 Mg4`), giving 7 vs 3, 2 vs 0 and 4 vs 0 structures. The one loss is Ga-Pt-Tm seed 43 (3 vs 5), where two re-plans moved the search away from a region that was about to pay off. With planning off, self-correction is the only steering: M1 proposes the full-system composition every query, the repetition trigger fires 6 times per run (108 in total), and that alone gives 5.11 per run, about the reference's level.

**AF and EF are 0 for every method.** That follows the official zero-baseline convention used by M2; they are discovery metrics, not wall-time speed-ups.

**Cost.** M3 uses no language model and the same 20 oracle queries. Mean wall time per run was 698 s for `m3_full` and 542 s for `m1_baseline` on this laptop, with 3 runs in parallel.

**Limits.** 18 pairs over 6 dev systems is a small sample, and the system-level pattern rests on 3 seeds per system. The check and hidden splits were not used, per M6's protocol.
